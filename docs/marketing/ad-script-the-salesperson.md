## Audio Mixing Notes

The clicks and keystrokes are the product. They must feel tactile, never buried.

**Levels**
- Music bed sits at **-20 LUFS**, roughly 12dB under the SFX. It is texture, not a lead.
- Keystroke peaks at **-8 dBFS**, mouse clicks at **-6 dBFS**. Clicks are the loudest thing in the film.
- Master to **-14 LUFS integrated**, true peak -1.5dB, for Facebook and Instagram.

**Making the SFX pop**
- **Sidechain the music** to every click and keystroke. 3 to 4dB of duck, 5ms attack, 180ms release. The music breathes around the interaction instead of fighting it.
- **Carve the music**, not the SFX. A gentle 3dB dip at 2.5–5kHz in the music bed leaves an empty pocket exactly where click transients live.
- **High-pass keystrokes at 180Hz** to strip the desk thud, then a 4dB presence lift around 5kHz for the crisp top edge.
- Give each click a **20–30ms room tail**. Bone-dry clicks sound synthetic; a hint of space makes them feel physically real.
- Vary keystroke samples and timing by ±15ms. Perfectly even typing reads as a machine and kills the ASMR effect.

**Non-negotiable**
- Mix once in **mono** and check on a phone speaker. Most of the audience hears it that way.
- **Design for muted playback.** Facebook autoplays silent, so the on-screen text must carry the entire message on its own. Treat sound as a reward for the people who unmute, never as the carrier of meaning.

## Colour Palette & Visual Direction

**Palette**

| Role | Selkirk act (light) | Nike act (dark) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground | `#FAFAFA` paper white | `#0B0B0B` near black |
| Ink | `#0A0A0A` | `#F5F5F5` |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` cards, 8% shadow | `#161616` cards |
| Muted text | `#6B6B6B` | `#A1A1A1` |
| Accent | The store's own brand colour | The store's own brand colour |
| Status | `#22C55E` green dot only | `#22C55E` green dot only |

Keep colour almost entirely out of the frame. The only saturation should be the
product photography and the single green "online" dot. That restraint is what
reads as premium, and it makes the accent colour land when it appears.

**Typography**
- Inter, SF Pro or Geist. Weight 500 for statements, 400 for supporting lines.
- Statement cards: 48–64px, tracking -2%, generous line height at 1.15.
- One idea per card. Never two sentences on screen at once.

**Cursor**
- Custom cursor at 140% scale with a soft 4px shadow so it reads on macro shots.
- Add a 3-frame trailing ghost at 20% opacity for direction, and ease every move
  with `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`. Cursors that move linearly look robotic.
- Always decelerate into a target and pause 120ms before clicking. The pause is
  what makes a click feel deliberate.

**UI highlighting**
- Radial spotlight follows the cursor: the focal element at 100%, everything else
  at 92% opacity with a 1px blur. Guides the eye without heavy vignetting.
- On focus, let the input's ring bloom over 200ms rather than snapping on.
- Frame the browser in a floating device mock, heavy soft shadow, no address bar
  or tab clutter. The UI should feel like it is floating in space.

**Motion**
- 300–400ms springs with slight overshoot for the panel and cards.
- 6–8px directional motion blur on transitions and scrolls.
- Hold every statement card for a minimum of 1.2s so it is readable at a scroll.
- 2–3% film grain across the whole piece for texture.

## Final Call To Action

**The shot.** Everything clears to near-black. The chat bubble icon draws itself
in the centre with a single stroke animation, then glides down to the
bottom-right corner where it lives on a real storefront. The wordmark fades in
beneath. Hold four seconds, longer than feels comfortable, so the URL is
readable and the last frame is what the algorithm freezes on.

**The overlay.**

> ### Add it to your store.
> Your catalogue. Your brand. Answering customers while you sleep.
>
> **See it live →** zy-commerce-demo.vercel.app
> *Built by Khor Ze Yi · AI chat for e-commerce*
>
> Demonstration stores. Not affiliated with any brand shown.

**Why this CTA.** It speaks to the store owner in their own terms: their
catalogue, their brand, their revenue while they are asleep. "See it live" beats
"Learn more" because the demo is genuinely clickable and instantly convincing.

## Production notes

**Capture settings**
- Record at 2560×1440, 60fps, then deliver 1080×1350 (4:5) for feed and 1080×1920
  for Reels. 4:5 occupies the most feed real estate.
- Browser at 150% zoom for the macro shots so text stays razor sharp when cropped.
- Record each act as a separate clean pass. Never try to perform the whole flow in
  one take.
- Type slowly and deliberately during capture. You can always speed-ramp in the
  edit, but you cannot invent detail that was never recorded.

**Honesty guardrails**
- Do not put invented statistics on screen, such as "increases sales 47%". You do
  not have that data yet and a store owner who asks for the source will find none.
  The demo is more persuasive than a fake number.
- Every price and product name in this script is real and currently live. If you
  re-shoot after the catalogue changes, update the script to match.
- **Do not show or imply a checkout.** The demo stores exist to show the
  assistant, and they deliberately sell nothing — the product page carries a
  disabled "Demo store — not for sale" button and the footer says the stores are
  not affiliated with any brand shown. Framing the film around reaching the right
  product, rather than reaching a purchase, is both honest and the stronger claim:
  choosing is the part the assistant actually does.
- Keep the storefronts recognisable as demonstrations. If a frame crops out the
  "Demo" badge in the header, that is fine; deliberately hiding it is not.

**Optional second cut, for a follow-up post**
A six-second clip showing someone typing "what's the weather today?" and the
assistant declining to answer, with the caption "It only ever talks about your
products." Brand safety is a real worry for store owners, but it is a defensive
message that would dilute the hero film. Give it its own post.
